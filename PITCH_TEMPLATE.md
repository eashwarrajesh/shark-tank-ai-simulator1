## 🎭 Understanding the Three Investor Personas

### 💰 Victor Cash - The Money Shark
**Focus**: REVENUE • COSTS • PROFIT

**What he cares about:**
- Exact revenue model and unit economics
- Break-even timeline and burn rate
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC) vs lifetime value (LTV)
- Proof that the business model actually works

**How to answer him:**
- Always include specific numbers (₹500/month, 15% commission, break-even in 8 months)
- Show you understand your costs (marketing, ops, tech)
- Explain how you make money and when you'll be profitable

---

### 🦈 Maya Street - The Customer Shark
**Focus**: CUSTOMERS • SWITCHING • ACQUISITION

**What she cares about:**
- Who exactly will pay for this
- Why they would switch from their current solution
- How you will acquire your first 100 customers
- Evidence of real customer demand

**How to answer her:**
- Name specific people or groups (not just "students" but "engineering students at IIT Delhi")
- Show you've talked to real potential customers
- Explain the pain that makes them switch

---

### 🚀 Rex Doubt - The Doubter Shark
**Focus**: RISK • COMPETITION • TEAM

**What he cares about:**
- What can go wrong and kill the startup
- Why Google/Facebook won't just copy this
- Why this team can actually execute
- Defensibility and competitive moats

**How to answer him:**
- Acknowledge risks honestly and show mitigation plans
- Explain your unfair advantage (network effects, exclusive data, patents)
- Prove your team has relevant experience or domain expertise

## 📊 Scoring System Explained

### Answer Scores (0-100 per round)

- **0-40**: Vague, no specifics, dodged the question
- **41-60**: Some detail but missing key evidence or numbers
- **61-80**: Good answer with specifics, but room for improvement
- **81-100**: Exceptional — specific numbers, named examples, solid evidence

### What Increases Your Score:
- ✅ Specific numbers (revenue, users, costs, timelines)
- ✅ Named examples (actual people, companies, or pilots)
- ✅ Evidence (pilot test results, customer interviews, market research)
- ✅ Concrete plans (not "we will do marketing" but "we will post in 5 college Facebook groups daily")

### What Decreases Your Score:
- ❌ Vague claims ("huge market", "everyone needs this")
- ❌ No numbers or timelines
- ❌ Dodging the question or pivoting to something else
- ❌ Generic answers that apply to any startup

## 🎯 Example Good vs Bad Answers

### Question: "How will you acquire your first 10 paying customers?"

**❌ Bad Answer (Score: 35/100)**
> "We will use social media marketing and word of mouth. Our product is so good that people will naturally want to share it. We'll also do some influencer partnerships and maybe some Facebook ads."

**Why it's bad**: No specifics, no timeline, no concrete plan, relies on hope.

---

**✅ Good Answer (Score: 78/100)**
> "We've already talked to 15 students in my college hostel. 8 said they'd pay ₹199/month if we launch next week. Our plan for the first 10 customers: (1) Convert those 8 pre-interested students in week 1, (2) Post in 3 specific college Facebook groups with 10,000+ members each, targeting exam season in week 2, (3) Offer the first month free to the first 50 signups to get testimonials. Based on our test post last week, we got 47 comments asking for the link."

**Why it's good**: Named specific people (8 students), specific numbers (₹199/month, 10k members), evidence (test post got 47 comments), and concrete timeline.

## 💡 Pro Tips for Strong Answers

### 1. Use the "Number-Name-Evidence" Framework
Every strong answer should include:
- **Number**: A specific metric (₹500/month, 10 customers, 3 months)
- **Name**: A specific example (IIT Delhi, John from our pilot, Zomato as competitor)
- **Evidence**: Proof that backs up your claim (pilot test, survey, market research)

### 2. Answer the ACTUAL Question
Don't pivot to what you want to talk about. If the investor asks about revenue, talk about revenue. If they ask about customers, talk about customers.

### 3. Be Honest About What You Don't Know
"I haven't calculated exact CAC yet, but based on our ₹5,000 Instagram ad test that got 200 clicks and 12 signups, CAC is around ₹400. I'm tracking this weekly." 

This is better than: "Our CAC will definitely be very low because our product is viral."

### 4. Use Real Examples from Your Research
- "I interviewed 30 students last month. 23 said they spend ₹800/month on restaurant food but miss home cooking."
- "We tested this with 5 landlords in Koramangala. 4 signed up for the paid plan after one month."

## 🚨 Common Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ Don't Say:
- "This is a huge market"
- "We'll go viral"
- "Everyone needs this"
- "We have no competitors"
- "First-mover advantage"
- "We'll figure it out"

### ✅ Instead Say:
- "The student food delivery market in Bangalore is ₹500 crore annually (source: RedSeer report 2025)"
- "Our referral rate in the pilot was 3 friends per user after 2 weeks"
- "Our target is 18-24 year old engineering students in tier 1 cities — about 2.5 million people"
- "Zomato and Swiggy exist, but their 25% commission makes hyperlocal home chefs unprofitable"
- "We're the first to report rent payments to credit bureaus, which takes 6 months of CIBIL integration"
- "We've mapped out customer acquisition for months 1-3 with specific tactics"

## 🔧 Mock Mode Configuration

The application automatically falls back to mock mode if the AI API is unavailable. Mock responses are:

- **Deterministic**: Same startup name + investor = same question every time
- **Score-based**: Answer quality affects the score (checks for numbers, length, specifics)
- **Realistic**: Uses a bank of 12 real investor questions (4 per investor type)

### Testing Mock Mode:
1. Leave `GROK_API_KEY` empty in `.env`
2. Or set `USE_MOCK=true` in `.env`
3. Server will log: `⚠️ Mock mode active`

## 📝 Customizing Templates

Want to add your own template? Use this structure:

```json
{
  "startupName": "YourStartup",
  "oneLiner": "One sentence explaining what you do",
  "problem": "2-3 sentences: Who suffers? What's broken? Why does it matter?",
  "solution": "2-3 sentences: What do you build? How does it solve the problem?",
  "targetCustomer": "Specific demographic + location + behavior",
  "businessModel": "How you make money + specific numbers",
  "competitiveAdvantage": "Why you win vs competitors + proof/evidence"
}
```

**Quality Checklist:**
- [ ] Startup name is memorable and relevant
- [ ] Problem is specific (not "people want better food")
- [ ] Solution is concrete (not "an app that helps")
- [ ] Target customer is narrow (not "everyone aged 18-60")
- [ ] Business model includes numbers (commission %, price, etc)
- [ ] Competitive advantage is defensible (not "better UX")

4. **What scored highest?** Double down on that strength
